📚 Physics Notes & References

Hively (US 9,306,527) requires pure gradient-driven (irrotational) currents for clean SLW. Bifilar coils cancel B and support only J=∇κ. High purity is needed for best match to a bifilar detector.

Nikolova & Zimmerman (QEX Jul/Aug 2011, US 8,165,531, 2007–2013 papers) demonstrated longitudinally polarized momentum / vector-potential waves. Documented ~1.5 km link at 1296 MHz using a waveguide-mounted transmit antenna and a biased fluorescent/plasma tube detector oriented to be relatively TEM-insensitive. Their patent shows a parabolic reflector + longitudinal dipole for directive longitudinal-A radiation.

Zimmerman noted that placing a high-gain horn (or equivalent) on the receive side of a working link should enable much longer ranges. With gain antennas at both ends and a selective detector, 10–20 km class distances are a reasonable exploratory target in this simulator (exact numbers depend on power, purity, frequency and noise).

  • Path loss uses a generalized free-space form with adjustable exponent (default 2) plus optional extra medium attenuation.
  • Purity factor multiplies Tx power that contributes to the usable longitudinal/potential mode. Low purity wastes power into ordinary EM that selective detectors largely reject.
  • Plasma detectors primarily sense the time-varying A; bifilar coils convert longitudinal E into gradient current.
  • Educational tool only — real links depend on many practical factors (matching, orientation, interference, exact detector response) not fully modeled here.
